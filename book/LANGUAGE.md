## The sentence has to be born in the language

Do not think the English sentence and then dress it. English syntax in Hebrew, Arabic, or Chinese is still English. A reader hears it.

Build the clause the way that language builds a clause.

- Hebrew: the verb can lead. Construct state is not an English "of". Don't stack adjectives in English order and call it a sentence.
- Arabic: the verb can lead. Idafa is not a chain of "of". A relative clause that exists only because the English had one should go. Case and agreement are part of the sentence, not a coat on an English frame.
- Chinese: topic, then comment, when that is the sentence. Don't reproduce English subordinate clauses with 的 piled until the line is English in characters. A four-character phrase is for when Chinese would use one.

Keep, in the target language's own writing:

- the date
- the place, named so a map can draw the area, not a dot
- the statute, the sum, the name
- a term that belongs to another language: the characters or the script, then a reading a reader of this translation can say

Do not add a sentence that tells the reader how the chapter connects to another chapter. The English book already has too many of those. Do not translate one into Hebrew, Arabic, or Chinese just because it was there.

If a line in the English is an AI-ism, do not spend skill making the AI-ism elegant in your language. Leave it plain, or skip the flourish. The note in `book/ai-isms/` is where that line gets named. Your job is the chapter as a piece of Hebrew, or Arabic, or Chinese.
