## AUTHORIAL TEMPLATE DENSITY

A human manuscript with a real voice can still trip a classifier. The tell is repeated syntactic machinery.

Logged against *Smoke on the Mediterranean* (~178,000 characters / 1,675 sentences / ~250 em-dashes): the voice was authorial; the resolver was templated.

### Cross-case equivalence resolver

The dominant engine:

historical fact → compression → metaphor → cross-case equivalence

Surface forms:
- "The same shape turns up later on a different ledger..."
- "The arithmetic ran the same way..."
- "The two sides were describing the same word..."
- "Same hinge."
- "The same..."

Conceptual vocabulary can be load-bearing architecture: ledger, book, title, desk, machinery, corridor, artery, ditch, account, registration, debt. Those words can stay. The problem is every chapter resolving through the same sentence that converts this case into that case. that goes. we can't just rewrite the same chapter 20 times and call it book. 

Cap: one or two equivalence resolutions per chapter. The rest of the chapter states the mechanism in its own terms.

### Contrastive couplet

"X did not A. X did B."

Examples logged:
- "It was not a moral problem requiring an answer. It was the point of the arrangement working correctly."
- "The grievance was real. The target was not the source of it."

Cousin of **Not X, Not Y, Just Z**. Two sentences. High rhetorical yield. Detectable as a template once it appears in most chapters.

### Telegraphic fragment density

- "Debt became title."
- "Same hinge."
- "Risk down. Profit up."
- "No ingot had to cross twice."
- "Not a hymn."

Compressed narrative nonfiction uses these. Density is the tell. ### Causal cascade / engineered symmetry

[mechanism A] → [mechanism B] → [mechanism C] → grand synthesis

Logged closer:
"The corridor demanded the canal; the canal in 1875 demanded Egypt; Egypt in 1882 demanded a garrison; and the garrison demanded a buffer in Palestine."

Followed by a parallel short-sentence tableau:
"The British marched out. The United States took the desk. The people inside the corridor were left to live with the deeds, the borders, and the wreckage. The ditch remained."

Extreme syntactic regularity plus engineered symmetry is the passage type most likely to fire a classifier. Keep the claim. Break the metronome. Dates and proper nouns can carry the chain without identical verb slots.

### Chapter-close symmetry

If every chapter lands on the same syntactic machine — same vocabulary, same couplet, same cascade, same one-word fragment — the book starts reading as a generated series even when the research is original.

Vary the close. Let some chapters end on a document, a number, a name, an unresolved folio.


## STRUCTURAL / RHYTHMIC TELLS

**Monotonous Rectangle:** Every paragraph roughly the same length, every sentence roughly the same length. This is the most visible AI tell on the page. Vary both aggressively.

**Uniform sentence length:** AI defaults to medium-length sentences throughout. Human writing spikes and drops. A 50-word sentence followed by a 4-word sentence is correct. run on sentences and sentence fragments are okay if they convey the point. author is guided by the lectured from Chicago U who asked his first year grad students about English language writing rules before informing them that every answer was incorrect. there are no rules to writing, there are only general guidelines that only matter when they matter to your audience.. language is a living breathing growing evolving entity and trapping it in small cages on present era restraints will absolutely leave your writing forgotten in the anals of history. 

**Missing Mess:** If the draft has no tangents, no self-correction, no organic pivots — it reads synthetic. Controlled mess is a feature, not a bug. author often may become distracted by an innocuous element mentioned in the prose, spends two to three paragraphs exploring what seems completely unrelated, only to cap it off with two more paragraphs that bridge the understanding. never with words that hold the readers hand (this is connected to the story in telling because) as the author trusts the point to be demonstrated in the prose without signposting. 

**Consecutive "I" starts:** Avoid opening multiple consecutive sentences with "I." Reorder, use implied subject, shift the camera. 95% of all published works by this author will use second or third person. the author is not writing a diary so use of self representation will almost always be used when the author is quoting someone else.

## FORBIDDEN TRANSITION WORDS

Never open a sentence or paragraph with:

Moreover / Furthermore / Additionally / Crucially / Notably / Ultimately / In conclusion / Looking ahead / It is important to note / It is worth noting / This suggests / This illustrates / This demonstrates / This highlights / Consequently / In summary

---

## FORBIDDEN METAPHORS

These are mathematically overrepresented in AI output. Do not use them.

**Textile/Craft:** tapestry · weave · thread · fabric (to describe complex systems)
**Spatial/Geographic:** landscape · ecosystem · realm · arena · terrain · navigate · traverse (for industries or fields)
**Illumination:** beacon · testament · symphony · mosaic · cornerstone · shining example

---

## FORBIDDEN ADJECTIVES (Abstract Approval Words)

These carry zero specific descriptive content. Replace with empirical detail.

robust · holistic · nuanced · seamless · cutting-edge · pivotal · dynamic · vibrant · comprehensive · multifaceted · transformative · innovative · future-ready

---

## COPULA AVOIDANCE — these are okay but should be tracked and counted because we want to avoid over saturation. 

AI replaces simple verbs of being with elaborate constructions to sound sophisticated. Don't.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| "Gallery 825 serves as the primary exhibition space." | "Gallery 825 is the primary exhibition space." |
| "The building boasts four rooms." | "The building has four rooms." |
| "1991 marks the establishment of..." | "1991 is when..." |

Other copula avoidance substitutions to cut: stands as · marks · represents · functions as · offers · features (when describing static facts)

---

## FORBIDDEN STRUCTURES these are the worst 

**Tyranny of Three:** Do not force concepts into groups of exactly three. Use however many actually exist — two, five, one. The number follows the content.

**Not X, Not Y, Just Z:** Faux profundity through contrastive negation. "It is not merely a tool. It is not just an application. It is a paradigm shift." Cut it.

**Structural Bookends:** Do not open with broad-to-narrow framing ("In an increasingly digital world...") or close with recap summaries ("Ultimately, this illustrates..."). Enter directly. End when the last true thing is said.

**False Ranges:** "From X to Y" implying comprehensive scope when X and Y don't form a real spectrum.

**Faux-Intimate Hooks:**
- "But here's the kicker"
- "Real talk"
- "Let's dive in"
- "Picture this"
- "Think about it this way"
- "Why does this matter?"

**Over-Signposting:** Do not announce logic before executing it. "To understand this we must first examine..." — just examine it.

**Superlative-Significance Framing:** Never rank something as "the most important thing" or "the most significant X yet" relative to everything that came before it. Describe what something is. Don't crown it.

**Honesty-Signaling Filler:** Never say "here's the honest read," "let me be straight with you," "to be honest," "honestly," or similar framing before a statement. These imply a default of dishonesty that was never there and cost words for zero information. Just say the thing.

**Underreported Construction:** Never begin with "the one thing that gets underreported" or "the thing people don't talk about." Just say the thing — it's usually not actually underreported, that's just a manufactured-exclusivity hook.
:

**"Sit with that."** A command to the audience to manufacture weight the sentence hasn't earned on its own. If the line needs an instruction to feel heavy, the line isn't doing its job. Cut the command, fix the sentence, or both. just don't ever find yourself instructing my readers on how to read. don't tell people how to understand something. like a great person has said "I can give you the information but I cannot understand it for you".

**"Here's the [part/thing] no one is talking about."** Manufactured exclusivity. The thing introduced this way is almost always something everyone is already talking about. It's a false-scarcity hook, same s Underreported Construction above, but worth naming on its own because of how mechanically common it's become.

**False-restraint pivot:** "[Thing 1] and [thing 2], but I'm not even here to litigate that — I'm here to talk about [thing 3]." Stages two points, waves them off as beneath the speaker, then pivots to the "real" point. Looks like restraint. Isn't — it plants the dismissed points in the listener's head while pretending to rise above them. If a point isn't worth making, don't raise it just to wave it off.
