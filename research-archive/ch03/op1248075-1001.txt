specially employed in the case, reports that from the inquiries made by him he 
fLls convinced that Mr. James Sadleir has not been in London since (nor for

office in July last. _ .

In addition to the papers sent to you on the 7th instant, with my report of 
the proceedings against Mr. James Sadleir, I beg herewith to send you some 
further documents, showing that in consequence of Mr. Sadleir’s continued 
absence the Judges of the several law courts have allowed service of orders in 
certain ’civil proceedings to be deemed good service on Mr. Sadleir, when 
served on Messrs. Kieriian & Tracey, of Lower Dominick-street, in this city, 
the attorney of Mr. James Sadleir.

A like Order in this Court in the cause of Carroll against Kennedy, and the 
Affidavit on which it was founded. 

A like Order in the cause of Walker against M ‘Dowell. 

1 did not think it necessary to wait to get the affidavit on which this latter 
order was founded. It was made by Mr. Thomas Fitzgerald, attorney for the 
plaintiff, to sustain an application to issue a scire facias on a judgment 
obtained against Mr. M'Dowell, the official manager, against Mr. James 
Sadleir, m.p.; and Mr. Fitzgerald states in his affidavit that “Mr. James 
Sadleir has left this country, and is now resident in either Norway or Sweden, 
as deponent is informed and believes, and as appears from affidavits in the 
Common Pleas, and Mr. Fitzgerald adds, that he believes that an order or 
process served upon Messrs. Kiernan & Tracey will be transmitted to Mr. James 
Sadleir. 

The copies of papers now sent are about the latest I can find. 

I beg also to send you a letter from Mr. Gason, the outgiving under-sheriff of 
the county of Tipperary, to show you that that gentleman made every exertion 
to have the writs in the outlawry proceedings served. 1 also saw Mr. Geo^e 
M‘Dowell, the official manager of the Tipperary Bank affairs, this day. He 
informs me that he believes that Mr. Sadleir left Ireland early in June last, and 
believes that he has not been in Ireland since. Mr. M'Dowell will be in Lon- 
don next week, as he has been summoned as a witness in the cause of Ginger 
V. Law, a case arising out of the Tipperary Bank business. 

I have, &c.
