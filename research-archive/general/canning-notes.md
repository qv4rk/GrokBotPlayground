## 0. Bottom line

- **Best dinner to stage:** the Sultan Abdülmecid's banquet at the new Dolmabahçe palace, July 1856. Fuad Pasha (Foreign Minister) and Âli Pasha (Grand Vizier) received the guests. Stratford presented the English guests and Thouvenel the French. Edmund Hornby, the British commissioner for the 1855 £5m war loan, had just been decorated for the Loan Commission and arrived in Stratford's state caique. A thunderstorm put out the gas, and someone joked "a second feast of Belshazzar". Both Hornbys describe it independently (§1A).
  - **UNVERIFIED:** the exact day. It falls between 17 and 24 July 1856; see §1A.
- **"The traveling guy raising money":** the best documented fit is **Edmund Hornby**. In August 1855 London sent him out to Constantinople to supervise the Anglo-French guaranteed £5m loan, in his own words "like a knight of finance". He dined with Stratford on the night he arrived, and he worked daily with Fuad, Âli and Reşid. Earlier travelling loan-raisers were Namık Pasha (sent to London and Paris at the end of 1853, and failed) and the Galata bankers Black & Durand (June 1854). Lord Hobart was *not* sent; Anderson says so explicitly (§1C).
- **Cousin's Irish land: CONFIRMED.** George Canning, 1st Baron Garvagh (1778–1840), was Stratford's first cousin. He inherited the Garvagh estate in Co. Londonderry in 1784 from his father Paul Canning, who was Stratford's uncle. The family also held land in Co. Cavan (Canningstown, Ballyhaise, Knockbride and Drumgoon parishes) through the Newburgh heiress. By 1876–83 the family held 15,406 acres (§3).
  - Stratford's own branch was disinherited. I found no Irish land owned by Stratford himself.
- **Direct Canning–Ireland statements: several found** (§2). Examples are the "Paddyland" letter of 1833, his 1833 praise of the Irish Coercion Bill, and a May 1853 despatch comparing Russia's demand to a French demand to protect "the Roman Catholic priesthood in Ireland". In his 1869 Lords speech he talks of "Irish provinces" and "land tenure".
  - **NOT FOUND:** any statement in which Stratford links Irish landlordism, rents, tithes or Irish title systems to Ottoman land or tax reform. The thesis link is not documented in anything I examined (§5).

## 1. Dinners and meetings: Stratford with Reşid, Âli and Fuad, 1853–58, and the war-loan men

### 1A. BEST SCENE: the Sultan's banquet at Dolmabahçe, July 1856

**Source 1: Lady [Emelia Bithynia] Hornby, *In and Around Stamboul*.** Philadelphia: James Challen & Son, 1858. Letter L, headed "Prinkipo, July 23d, 1856", pp. 402–406. https://archive.org/details/inaroundstamboul00hornrich
> "It is a great pleasure to know that the Sultan is satisfied with the course which the Commissioners have pursued with regard to the English Loan." (p. 402; this is about Edmund Hornby receiving the Medjidie)

> "Last Thursday the Sultan's grand dinner-party 'came off' at the new Palace of Dolma Batche ... all but the principal guest, Sir E. Codrington, who found it impossible to land ... Edmund was among the fortunate people who rowed to the beautiful white-marble steps and gate of Dolma Batche with Lord Stratford and staff, in the magnificent state caique ... The company were received by the Minister of Foreign Affairs, Fuad Pasha, and by Aali Pasha, the Grand Vizier ... Lord Stratford presented the English guests, and M. de Thouvenel the French ... The dragoman, Count Stefano Pisani, translated ... The chandelier ... cost an immense sum of money ... it burns four hundred jets of gas ... the pashas did eat of every thing ... the musicians grew frightened at the storm ... and ran away ... the rare dessert was demolished almost in the dark by Turk and Christian ... the first whom the world ever saw assembled in the palace of a Sultan ... it only wanted 'the writing on the wall' ... Omar Pasha was among the Turkish generals. The dinner was over by half-past nine. The next day Lord Stratford gave a grand military dinner at Pera. Lord Lyons, Sir Edward Codrington ... were there." (pp. 403–405; the ellipses are my abridgements)

**Source 2: Lady Hornby, *Constantinople during the Crimean War*.** London: Richard Bentley, 1863. https://archive.org/details/constantinopled00unkngoog
- A letter headed "Prinkipo, July 23rd, 1856" (c. p. 402ff) says:
> "Yesterday the Sultan's dinner-party came off. It has been the talk of Constantinople for the last three weeks. Famous cooks and waiters ... from Paris ... not enough [chairs], by ten, for the guests invited (one hundred and thirty)"
- The same letter says "It was Tuesday" and "there is a grand dinner-party at the Embassy to-day to all those invited by the Sultan yesterday."
- The next letter, LIV, has an OCR date of "July 28rd, 1666" [sic, OCR]. It begins "Last Thursday the Sultan's grand dinner-party ..." and runs pp. 407–411.

**Source 3: Edmund Hornby, *Sir Edmund Hornby: An Autobiography*.** London: Constable, 1929. pp. 82–83. https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.206554
- He gives about 250 persons at the Sultan's banquet. Hornby "sat between General Pelissier ... and General Codrington".
- The Sultan "proposed and drank a toast, and then left". Then came a thunderstorm and the gas went out.
- The Belgian Minister's quip: "a second feast of Belshazzar".

**Discrepancies (UNVERIFIED):**
- Date: Tuesday 22 July 1856 or a Thursday (17 or 24 July 1856). The letters contradict each other and no newspaper report has been checked. A *Times* or *Illustrated London News* report from late July or August 1856 would settle it.
- Headcount: 130 (Lady Hornby) or about 250 (Hornby).
- Codrington: Lady Hornby says he could not land, but Hornby has himself seated beside Codrington.
- Hornby wrote his account about 70 years later. Lady Hornby's letters are contemporary and should be preferred.

**Money talk around this dinner (Lady Hornby, Philadelphia ed.):**
- The Loan Commission resolved "to prevent the money of the loan being spent on diamond necklaces or new slaves". Turkish ministers "had obtained advances from their Saraffs at high interest, in anticipation of their share of the plunder". (Letter XII, Therapia, 10 Nov 1855, c. p. 105; the 1863 ed. has it at c. pp. 94–96.)
- A minister of finance "absolutely alters the value of the paper money".
- Provincial judges "sell or let their places to the highest bidder ... who extorts a living, by extra imposts or taxes".
- "our guest's" talk of "poor olive-growers and unfortunate farmers of the provinces, taxed, robbed, and ruined by pashas reveling in palaces on the Bosphorus", and of the Sultan's debts and the Minister of Finance. (pp. 294–295)

### 1B. Alternative documented dinners

1. **Hornby's first dinner at the Embassy, Therapia, early September 1855.**
   - Source: Hornby, *Autobiography*, pp. 67–69. The footnote dates his appointment to 30 Aug 1855.
   - Appointment: "asked if I would go out to Constantinople to manage the loan of five millions which France and England had granted to the Turks to enable them to carry on the Crimean War ... above all to see that it went to the troops and was not spent on palaces, harems, and bacsheesh. Thus fully accoutred, I sallied forth like a knight of finance." (p. 67)
   - The morning interview: Stratford was "in his grey dressing gown" and angry that his own nominee, "a Greek and competent financier", had not been appointed.
   - Dinner: "Lord Stratford took my wife in to dinner. There was only the staff of the Embassy present — Lord Napier ... Alison, the Oriental Secretary; Count Pisani ... Colonel Mansfield ... Lord and Lady George Paget".
   - After dinner Stratford said: "I am afraid — nay indeed I am certain — I was most unjustly rude to you this morning. Will you accept an old man's apology who has much to trouble him?"
   - Limitation: no pashas were present.
2. **Stratford's advice to Hornby on dealing with the pashas.** Same source, pp. 73–74:
   > "Ask him for a million, and he will readily promise it; ask him for £100 ... he will spend months ... proving to you that he cannot get it. Don't assume that men like Reschid, Ali or Fuad Pashas are trying to cheat you".
   - Reşid on Stratford (p. 72): "'He is often overbearing,' said old Reschid Pasha to me one day, 'but we know he is a true friend to Turkey and has more pluck in his little finger than the whole Divan put together.'"
   - p. 76: "with the Ministers I got on well enough, especially Fuad, Ali, old Mehemet, and Kiani".
   - p. 77: "I do not believe that more than half a million was misapplied". The same page has the bag that should have held £20,000 in gold and was found full of copper coins.
   - p. 78: the banker Theodore Baltazzi ("Batazzi") offered Hornby a £1,250 cheque, which he refused.
   - pp. 113–114: "I must in justice to Fuad Pasha and Ali Pasha ... bear witness to their clean-handedness. At their and Lord Stratford's request I examined all the proposals" (the 1856 concession mania).
3. **Christmas dinner at the British Embassy, Pera, 25 Dec 1855.** The Hornbys were guests of Lord and Lady Stratford: "Lord and Lady Stratford kindly taking pity on our loneliness, and asking us to dinner." Source: Lady Hornby, *In and Around Stamboul* (Phila. 1858), pp. 152–159. The guests were mostly officers, and no pashas are named.
4. **Fancy ball (bal costumé) at the British Embassy, late Jan / early Feb 1856.** The Sultan attended, along with Âli and Fuad.
   - Lady Hornby, *In and Around Stamboul* (Phila. 1858), Letter XXV, "Orta-kioy, February 8th, 1856", pp. 206–229, esp. 220–224. 1863 ed. pp. 206–211.
   - "Mehemet Ali, Aali Pasha, the Grand Vizier, and most of the pashas, remained almost to the last"
   - "The pashas eat enormously at a ball ... drink vast quantities of champagne ... call it 'eau gazeuse.'"
   - Fuad unmasked Percy Smythe, who was disguised as a Turkish lady (p. 223).
   - "Lord Stratford asked Edmund very kindly to be one of his staff, who met the Sultan at the entrance" (pp. 228–229).
   - Hornby, *Autobiography*, pp. 83–84: "Ali Pasha gave up the struggle and besought Lord Stratford's interference".
   - Lane-Poole, ii. 444–445: "For the first time in the history of Turkey a Sultan was the guest of a Christian ambassador. Lady Stratford was giving a bal costumé". Lane-Poole's margin cites "In and Around Stambul, i. 254 ff."
   - **UNVERIFIED:** the exact date. Lady Hornby's letter of 8 Feb describes it; Lane-Poole says February 1856.
5. **Nassau W. Senior dines with Reşid Pasha, Tuesday 6 Oct 1857.**
   - Source: Senior, *A Journal Kept in Turkey and Greece in the Autumn of 1857 and the Beginning of 1858* (London: Longman, 1859), pp. 53–58. https://archive.org/details/ajournalkeptint00senigoog

### 1C. Who raised the war money: scholarly frame

Source: Olive Anderson, "Great Britain and the Beginnings of the Ottoman Public Debt, 1854–55", *Historical Journal* 7:1 (1964), pp. 47–63. PDF copy: https://psi427.cankaya.edu.tr/uploads/files/Anderson%2C%20GB%20and%20Beginnings%20of%20the%20Ott%20Publi%20Debt%2C%201854-1855%20%281964%29.pdf

- **Namık Pasha:** "at the end of 1853 the Porte dispatched Namik Pasha to effect a loan in London and Paris." He insisted on an issue price of 95 and failed. Clarendon, 13 Mar 1854: "Any man of ordinary experience or capacity would have got the money long ago". (p. 48)
- **Stratford to Clarendon, 7 Apr 1854 (FO 352/38), quoted p. 48:**
  > "The Sultan told me the other day that he had consented in time of war to a Loan which he had thought it right to stand out (sic) in time of peace. In this as in other matters, necessity is the only effective lever against Islamism."
- **Rothschild:** tried and failed to raise a loan at 85, 22–29 Mar 1854. (p. 49)
- **Black and Durand:** "two foreign merchant bankers of Galata, Messrs Black and Durand" were sent as negotiators in June 1854. (pp. 49–50)
- **The 1854 loan:** Goldsmid & Palmer raised a 6% loan at 80 on Clarendon's certificate in August 1854. (p. 51)
  - Stratford "had been bitterly disappointed by the contract". (p. 52)
  - Stratford on the 1854 proceeds: "There is no 'bank' wherein to place the produce of the Loan". (p. 53)
- **The 1855 guaranteed loan:** Fuad and Âli replaced Reşid on 2 May 1855 and pushed the £5m guaranteed loan (p. 54). Rothschild's tender came in at 102½ (p. 58).
- **Lord Hobart:** p. 59 n. 67: "Lord Hobart was not sent on this occasion (as stated by Blaisdell, p. 28 and Du Velay, p. 142)." Anderson calls Hornby Stratford's "new-found ally".
- **Stratford to Clarendon, no. 152, 8 Feb 1856, quoted p. 61:**
  > "In procuring the establishment of the first Loan Commission I had in view not only a strict application of the proceeds of the Loan to their intended object, but also the introduction of a wholesome foreign agency into the financial operations of the Porte to serve as a precedent for its extension to other departments of the administration where it is equally indispensable."
- **Corroboration:** Hansard, Commons, 21 July 1876, "The Turkish Debt — The Loan of 1854". The 1854 general bond was signed by J. N. Black, Vely, P. Durand and C. Musurus. https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1876/jul/21/the-turkish-debt-the-loan-of-1854
- **Stratford on money, 1854.** Lane-Poole ii. 354, to Clarendon, 12 June 1854: "the conclusion of a loan in fair prospect".

## 2. Stratford Canning on Ireland: direct statements

   > "Your friends are quite right in not viewing the Catholic question — I mean the *new* one — through party spectacles. It is emphatically *national*. The closest items are No. 8 (the Ireland analogy, which is about religious protection, not land) and No. 11 (Irish "land tenure", 1869, well after 1858).

## 3. The cousin's Irish land: verified

**Burke's *Peerage and Baronetage*, 31st ed. (London, 1869), p. 481, "GARVAGH".** https://archive.org/details/genealogicalhera00inburk
> "George Canninge, Esq. of Coleraine, co. Derry, had a grant of the manor of Garvagh by patent from Queen Elizabeth, and settled there. He had also a lease from the Ironmongers' Company of their estate of Agivey, co. Derry, which expired about the year 1780."

> "Stratford Canning, Esq. of Garvagh, b. in 1703 ... m. 1734, Letitia, dau. and heir of Obadiah Newburgh, Esq. of Ballyhaise, co. Cavan"

Sons:
- "I. George ... having incurred the displeasure of his father, and the penalty of disinheritance, by an improvident marriage". He was the father of George Canning the Prime Minister.
- "II. Paul, who s. at Garvagh"
- "III. Stratford, a merchant of London, who left at his decease, in 1787 ... 4 Stratford (the Right Hon. Sir) ... ambassador to the Porte"

> "Mr. Canning d. 3 Sept. 1775, and was s. by his 2nd son, Paul Canning, Esq. of Garvagh ... s. at his decease, Nov. 1784, by his only son, George Canning, Esq. of Garvagh, F.R.S. and F.A.S., b. 15 Nov. 1778, who was raised to the peerage, 28 Oct. 1818, as Baron Garvagh ... m. 1st, 13 July, 1803, Lady Georgiana Stewart, 4th dau. of Robert, 1st Marquess of Londonderry ... lord-lieut. of the co. of Londonderry, d. 20 Aug. 1840 ... Seats—Garvagh, co. Londonderry, and Canningstown, co. Cavan."

**Conclusion.** Stratford's grandfather, Stratford Canning of Garvagh (1703–75), held the Derry and Cavan land. It passed to Paul Canning (Stratford's uncle) and in 1784 to Paul's son George, later 1st Baron Garvagh (1778–1840), Stratford's first cousin. Stratford's father and George Canning's father were disinherited or landless younger branches.

**Discrepancy:** Burke says the Garvagh grant came from Queen Elizabeth; Lane-Poole and the Ironmongers'-agent tradition say James I, 1618. **UNVERIFIED** which is correct; this needs PRONI or the Ironmongers' Company records.

## 4. British advice and influence on Ottoman land and tax reform before 1858

1. **Stratford's reform programme, 1849.** Despatch clxiv, 30 Nov 1849, summarised by Lane-Poole, ii. 208. The Elchi's reforms were:
   > "(1) the abolition of the capitation tax levied on Christians, and their admission into the army; (2) Christian evidence to be allowed in the law courts; (3) more Christians to be elected to local councils; (4) the revenue to be collected in a more equitable manner."
2. **Tax-farming, 1850.**
   - Despatch cclxi, 22 Aug 1850 (Lane-Poole ii. 210): "corrupt practices in office, a low revenue, high prices, a pernicious system of recruiting the army and a worse one of farming the taxes".
   - To Palmerston, 1850 (ii. 213): "...unless the finances be restored to order, and a real practical system of reform ... be frankly adopted." Also on Reşid: his "reputation for being superior to the temptations of money is fast melting away under the sunny influence of his magnificent buildings and still more magnificent investments."
   - To Layard, 1850 (ii. 213–214): "...prospectively the cessation of the far more mischievous custom of farming out the public revenues which has been promised and confidentially announced to me. The per contra list of corruption, peculation, insurrection, massacres, pillages and financial disorder ... The deficit in the revenue is serious".
   - Lane-Poole's own summary (ii. 215): "Reforms cost money, but the Turkish treasury was just then in sore straits ... To make roads, build batteries, remove venal officials, and reform the taxation would necessitate a foreign loan, and usury is against the Mohammedan law."
3. **London instructs Stratford to press land-tenure reform, January 1855.** Clarendon to Stratford, FO, 26 Jan 1855. FO CP, *Rights and Privileges ...*, Part VI (printed 15 Jan 1856), No. 134, pp. 124–125 (FO 424/15). It draws on Consul-General Moore (Beirut), despatch No. 62 of 19 Dec [1854], on Syria's agricultural resources.
   > "...admitting Christians to acquire landed property, or by encouraging [immigration] from foreign countries, or by holding out inducements to foreign capitalists to invest their money, either directly or indirectly, in Syria ... it is unquestionable that if the Turkish Government could be induced to modify its laws in regard to the tenour of landed property, and to lay aside its distrust, not only of its own Christian subjects, but of others who may desire to settle in the Turkish dominions, a very decided improvement in the strength and resources of the Empire would result ... Your Excellency will press these considerations upon the Porte on all suitable occasions."
4. **Drafting the Hatt-ı Hümayun, January 1856: land for foreigners.** FO CP, *Correspondence respecting Christian Privileges in Turkey 1851–1856* (printed 1 Apr 1856), FO 424/9.
   - No. 164, Stratford to Clarendon, No. 100, 28 Jan 1856, p. 152:
     > "The points from which they were most inclined to recede, are, those of religious persecution; equal admissibility to office; and the possession of landed property by foreigners ... On the question of landed property I made a statement calculated to remove every doubt of any difference of opinion between me and my colleagues".
     He reports that the Council sanctioned the joint propositions "with the exception of what concerns the possession of landed property by foreigners, and the procedure in Civil Courts."
   - Inclosure 1, Stratford to Pisani, 25 Jan 1856, p. 153 (French original):
     > "Il m'est revenu que je suis supposé d'attacher moins d'importance que les Représentants de France et d'Autriche à ce que la permission de posséder des biens fonds en Turquie soit accordée aux étrangers. J'ai à cœur de faire savoir que cette supposition est tout-à-fait erronée. Je suis convaincu, au contraire, que le rapprochement cordial de la Turquie à l'Europe est essentiel au bien-être et au maintien de l'Empire Ottoman, et que la mesure la plus efficace dans ce sens ne serait autre que la faculté accordée aux étrangers d'y posséder des immeubles de toute espèce."
   - No. 170, Clarendon to Stratford, No. 174, FO, 13 Feb 1856, p. 159:
     > "As regards the privilege of holding land, Her Majesty's Government consider it of vast importance to Turkey, as there is reason to believe that a large amount of capital is ready for investment in land, upon proper guarantees as to tenure, whenever peace is restored."
   - A later document in the same volume, p. 183: "the action of commerce and the new laws for the tenure of land will bring the rivals into close contact."
   - The same volume contains French text on the "fermiers des dîmes (Multezim)" (tithe-farmers). Not yet transcribed.
   - Scholarly frame: Roderic Davison, *Reform in the Ottoman Empire 1856–1876* (Princeton, 1963), ch. 2, on the January 1856 conferences of Stratford, Thouvenel and Prokesch with Âli, Fuad and Kallimaki; it cites FO 78/1173, Stratford to Clarendon #213, 21 Feb 1856.
5. **The Hatt-ı Hümayun (18 Feb 1856; Lane-Poole says the 21st).** Lane-Poole ii. 440–441:
   > "the absolute equality of taxation among the different classes of the population without distinction of creed; the abolition of the system of farming the taxes ... Lord Stratford's hand is traceable in every line"
   Stratford's own words: "Considering that of the five persons who joined me in drawing up the Charter, two were Mohammedans, two Roman Catholics, and one a member of the Greek Church, its acceptance was little short of a miracle."
   - Senior's summary (*Journal*, 1859, Preface pp. x–xi):
     > "15. All foreigners may possess landed property, obeying the laws, and paying the taxes ... 16. All persons ... shall pay the same taxes. The abuses in the exaction of taxes, particularly of the tithes, shall be reformed. Direct payment into the exchequer shall be substituted for the system of farms. 17. Whilst farms exist they shall be granted publicly to the highest bidder, and official persons, particularly the medjils, are prohibited from becoming farmers."
6. **Stratford's retrospective, 1877.** Stratford, "Turkey", *The Nineteenth Century* vol. i (June 1877), as quoted by James Finn, *Stirring Times* (London, 1878), vol. i, pp. 179–180 note. https://archive.org/details/stirringtimes01finn
   > "By one the faculty of holding land in fee throughout Turkey was granted to foreign subjects, with a reserve of some preliminary arrangements. By the other both natives and foreigners were allowed full liberty of conscience". (Finn gives it as *Nineteenth Century* p. 731)
   - **UNVERIFIED** against the original magazine page. The volume is full view on HathiTrust (catalog 006061863), and the article was reprinted in Stratford's *The Eastern Question* (1881).
7. **Senior's 1857 evidence on land, rents and tax-farms at Constantinople** (*Journal*, 1859):
   - pp. 23–24, informant A.B.:
     > "You are not allowed to buy, and the Turks will let only from year to year ... if you improve it, your landlord raises the rent on every renewal. Mr. Cumberbatch, the British consul at Pera, pays twice as much now ... 'The Hatt-i-Humâyoon,' I said, 'declares that foreigners may purchase land and houses.' 'Yes,' he answered, 'but with this reserve, that the details are to be arranged between the Sultan and the foreign governments ...'"
     Mr. Hanson's Candilli property was bought "in the name of his wife ... a Christian rajah ... During Lord Stratford's reign Mr. Hanson is perfectly safe".
   - pp. 134–135:
     > "Reschid Pasha's reforms, the direct collection of the property tax, which before his time was farmed, and the abolition of monopolies, and of the internal customs and octrois, gave a great impulse to agriculture ... The revenue, when he began his reforms, was about five millions sterling. It fell at once to two and a half; but the next year it rose to six, then to seven, and now it is between eight and nine."
   - p. 148, informant C.D.:
     > "fund the floating debt ... putting an end to the madness of palace-building, and by substituting direct collection of the revenue for the present system of farms. I have no doubt that the revenue, if honestly and directly collected, could be doubled."
   - pp. 210–211, informant Y.:
     > "No Turk is an improving landlord, or even a repairing landlord ... The only considerable enterprise in which he ever engages is the farming some branch of the public revenue."
   - p. 88: "Lord Stratford really wishes to improve Turkey; but he is an exception to all rules."
8. **Palestine.** Finn, *Stirring Times*, vol. i, p. 217: the fellahin suffered "the exactions and impositions of the tax-farmers, who were often Christians". The "TAX-FARMING EXEMPLIFIED" section (c. vol. i, p. 285) is not yet read.
9. **Hornby on property law.** *Autobiography*, p. 95: Fuad helped him lease a Bosphorus house; "All those on both sides were reserved for the Turks, no Christians being allowed to own or rent any in so-called Mohammedan quarters."
10. **Ottoman landlords in Lane-Poole.** Lane-Poole i. 396: Mahmud "had treated the Derebeys, or great hereditary landlords, in a similar manner."

## 5. Thesis check: is there an Irish landlordism → 1858 Land Code link?

- Documented: Stratford pressed hard for (a) the abolition of tax-farming (iltizam) and direct collection of revenue, 1849–56, and (b) the right of foreigners to own land, 1855–56, on London's instructions (Clarendon, 26 Jan 1855 and 13 Feb 1856). London's stated motive was to open Ottoman land to foreign (British) capital "upon proper guarantees as to tenure".
- Documented: his family's wealth came from an Ulster plantation estate (Garvagh). His own branch was disinherited and his mother was Irish (Dublin merchant family).
- **NOT documented in anything examined:**
  - any Stratford statement drawing on Irish landlord, rent, tithe or registry practice for Ottoman reform;
  - any Stratford role in drafting the Land Code of 21 April 1858 (7 Ramazan 1274) or the tapu regulations. Stratford left Constantinople for good in late 1857 / early 1858.
- On the thesis as stated, the honest framing is inference or analogy, not documented transmission.
- Possible lead (not pursued): Temperley, "The Last Phase of Stratford de Redcliffe, 1855–58", *English Historical Review* 47 (1932), pp. 216–231, may cover the 1857–58 reform agenda.

## 6. Strongest primary sources (ranked)

1. **Foreign Office Confidential Print, FO 424/8, 9 and 15.** Stratford–Clarendon despatches, 1853–56: the Ireland analogy (22 May 1853), land for foreigners (25 and 28 Jan 1856, 13 Feb 1856), and land-tenure instructions (26 Jan 1855). https://archive.org/details/turkey-1841-1957
2. **Lady Hornby's letters.** *In and Around Stamboul* (Philadelphia, 1858; https://archive.org/details/inaroundstamboul00hornrich) and *Constantinople during the Crimean War* (London, 1863; https://archive.org/details/constantinopled00unkngoog). These are contemporary eyewitness accounts of the Dolmabahçe dinner, the Embassy ball, the Christmas dinner and the loan commission.

4. **Lane-Poole, *Life of Stratford Canning*** (1888). It prints Stratford's private letters (Paddyland, Coercion Bill, tax-farming) and his Memoirs on the family's Garvagh origin. https://archive.org/details/lifeofrighthonou01lane and https://archive.org/details/lifeofrighthon02lane

Plus Hansard for the 1836, 1840 and 1869 speeches, and Burke 1869 and Bateman 1883 for Garvagh.
