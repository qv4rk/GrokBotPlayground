WITH respect to your Lordship’s despatches Nog. 465 and 612 of the 
Lith anc 1Sth of September, my shorcege way of answering them would 
be to give your Lordship in as few words as possible my ideas as to the 
general state of the Turkish finances, as to what can be done at the 
present time about them, as to what has been done. 

|. In regard to ascertaining the revenue and forming an estimate of 
the expenses according to the existing system, this, after much trouble 
and difficulty from the want of accurate accounts, has been at last 
effected, 

2. The control which your Lordship suggests lias been in a certain 
degree provided for by means of a Mixed Kuropean Commission, which, 
however, is formed on a wrong basis, as | pointed out at the time, and 
must be improved. 

3. The establishment and carrying out of a better system of percep- 
tion ean only be done by a proper Ministry of Finance: this I consider as 
a sine gud non, and no BARE but one which has the greater number of its 
principal employés Buropeans can sullice; of this Lamsure. The Turkish 
Government neither refuses nor assents, 

4, The revenue may be considered at the present time to be at about 
10,000,000/ or 11,000,0002. The expenditure may be taken at something 
over; but | am really afraid of stating afigure. | have sometimes been 
told they balaneed, and figures shown are to that elfeet; at others, an

The expenses by the control I have pointed out, if efficient, would 
be diminished by, at least, 2,000,000. or 2,500,000. 

The receipts by a proper Ministry of Finance would be increased in 
the same proportion. ion 

[t is on this fund that the State would have principally to count in 
its subsequent atrangements, and this is why a proper Ministry of Finance 
is an absolute necessity. : 

I now come to the debts, which throw the Government at this moment 
into such confusion. 

I will take them at about 18,000,0001. sterling. ‘This would include 
claims not yet arranged, set down at 2.000,000/. ; the Government how says 
only 16,000,000!, exclusive of foreign debts. eaten 

I must then separate these 18,000,000L into two divisions, one of 
12,000,000/., the other of 6,000,0001. First, these 12,000,000/ have to be paid: 
it is clear they cannot be, but an interest of 7 per cent. on them might. 
The capital, therefore, must be converted into a debt, with interest, and if 
this interest is duly secured it assumes the value of capital, 

The 6,000,000 remaining, however, which | have named as a debt are 
not in fact a debt, but a debt that has been paid and Jeft a vacuum in the 
treasury which has to be supplied. This vacuum has been created in. the 
following manner: The Government has placed the revenue of the next 
seven months out of its power. It has made it over for sums received and 
spent. ‘Thus the whole of the expenses of the State have to be paid for 
the next seven months, and there is nothing to pay them, Indeed, for the 
last two or three months there has been nothing but what has been found 
by short loans at 45 or 40 per cent. 

* Tt is this 6,000,000, whichit was wished to borrow. [fa loan cannot 
be made, what is to be done? You may refuse to pay other creditors and 
eo on, but you cannot refuse to pay the army, navy, and civil functionaries, 
or the attributes and action of a Government cease. A simple way would 
be to make some arrangement with those to whom the revenue has been 
thus alienated, and to spread the sum they have now to receive over a 
series of years. = 

This, of course, has been thought of, but found impossible, ‘The 
ereditors will not consent to relinquish the obligations they hold. They 
wot the money, in fact, advanced on bills at short date “it these bills are 
not paid,—and they cannot be paid if they relinquish the receipts which 
were toacquit them,—it entails immense losses, perhaps bankruptey, on all 
the various houses whieh have dealt in these transactions, and 4,000,000/. 
or 5,000,000/ would, | hear, full on English houses. 

This then not being possible —for it was the lirst thing to think of— 
where are the 6,000,000/ to come from? ‘There is no money in the 
treasury. In the present condition of the Empire any extraordinary 
amount of taxation for the purpose would probably excite universal 
insurreetion, for it would unite all classes and sects in a common cause ; 
besides, the returns from taxation would be slow—the want is immediate. 
After thinking over every other method, the only one [ can see is the follow- 
ing one—a forced loan for about 1,000,000/,, or 1,500,000, which is ‘all 
the country would bear. Lt might be taken in plate, and exacted from 
men ofall ranks who are in employment. But there would still remain 
5,000,0001, My notion in respect to these would be this: to place the 
State property easily disposable, which the Government only values at 
about 1,000,000. sterling,* in the hands of a Mixed Commission, which 
should issue treasury bonds. ‘The existing caimés should be taken up by 
these bonds,f which would be received in payment of the State property 
(sold always by competition), and 5,000,000/. of new paper money would 
then be issued. 

lt is to be remembered that this sum, either in metallic or in paper, 
is in a certain degree required for the ordinary traffie to replace the 
5,500,000 of caimés which have been recalled, and for which no substitute of 
any Kind has been created, Butin issuing this new paper it must be kepr

The new paper (which would only circulate in and about the capital)

There is much, doubtless, to say against such a project, but I see no 
other better; for no other can meet the next six months’ expenditure ; and 
unless this can be met it is impossible to think of the other reforms ; for 
the Turks are so far right when they say “unless we can pay our army, 
and navy, and functionaries to-day, how can we sit down and make plans 
for to-morrow?” 

All, however, who are in the habit of lending money at high interest 
and for short terms will oppose a general scheme which is to meet the 
general embarrassments. 

They would prefer renewing from three months to three months their 
present loans, and thus sink the country for their own profit daily and 
daily deeper in the gulf in which it would finally and irretrievably be lost. 
But in my opinion one great and decisive measure, comprehending the 
whole condition of things, is the only one to adopt. 

I have dealt purposely in generalities, without entering into figures or 
details, in order to give as broad and clear a view as ssible of the actual 
state of things, and explain why the 6,000,0001,, so olten talked of, have to 
be found somehow, and that immediately ; and why | think a new paper 
issue, as under the regulations | have laid down, the old paper money 
being recalled, is not a bad expedient, A loan at moderate interest was 
the simplest mode: and when that is got this paper money may be, if 
advisable, withdrawn: but that loan being now impossible, the measure | 
have suggested seems to me somewhat ingenious, and not impracticable. 
Having, however, put it before the Porte for consideration, | do not urge 
its adoption, for the success of any measure will depend on its mode of 
execution, and as over this I can haye no control, | cannot be sanguine 
as to any result, 

As your Lordship will pereeive, the whole value of the plan of consoli- 
datin the other outstanding engagements must depend on changing the 
actual deficit intoa surplus. Nothing would be easier in skilful and hones 
hands. But credit can only be re-established when these are found, 

The Turks are wholly ignorant in financial matters, and such an 
education as that required is not given in a moment. The Greeks and 
Armenians, though clever in commerce and speculation, are as ignorant as 
the Turks as to the general principles of finance and financial administra- 
tion; consequently the revival of ‘Turkish credit and the employment 
of foreigners in the financial departments are in my opinion, as 1 have 
frequently and now repeat, inseparable.

he Undersigned has further the honour to state to Aali Pasha, with 
regard to the present Budget, that the principal facts connected with it 
do not appear to be stated with sufficient clearness, and the manner in 
which the proposed reduction of a million and a half in the expenditure ts 
to be effected, is not stated at all. Noallowance has been made for claims 
not yet decided upon, which will amount to & considerable sum, nor is it 
clear whether the arrears of pay due to the army are included in the 
statement of the expenditure to be mace. 

The Budget is, moreover, altogether a fallacious one, as far as the 
statement of income 1s concerned, inasmuch as the revenue for several 
months, if not for a whole year, is made over by anticipation as security 
for various loans raised and already spent. It appears therefore— 

lst, That the sum now borrowed 1s ‘nsuflicient to meet the urgent 
demands of the Porte. 

Qndly. That the Porte has added nearly 1,500,0001, sterling to the 
expenditure side of the account on a balance sheet which only brings out 
a tolerable equality between receipt and expenditure by the statement of 
1,500,000! of unspecified reductions. And, 

ordly. ‘That, notwithstanding this very unsatisfactory state of things, 
nothing would beso easy as far as the Turkish Government to restore its 
finances by skilful and prudent management, 

The observations which M, de Pleue makes with regard to the 
finances of Greece, and the inferences which he draws from his experience 
in that country, confirm this opinion. 

But without such good and improved management this new loan will 
only be a further step towards that bunkruptey with which the Turkish 
State is threatened ; and it 18, therefore, to be feared that at the end of 
another year the Porte may be not merely in the same pecuniary embar- 
ragsment as at present, but will have the further difficulty of having to 
furnish the amount required for the interest of this new loan, 

The Government of Her Britannic Majesty. in directing the Under- 
signed to press stron ly on the notice of the Sublime Porte the absolute 
necessity of introducing better management into its financial system, 18 
netuated solely by the desire which it has so constantly evinced to promote 
the welfare and prosperity of the Empire of the Sultan. 

Her Majesty's Government feel it to be their duty to warn the Porte 
strongly of the disastrous consequences which must inevitably follow a 

yerseverance in the improvident system of financial administration which 
vas led the Turkish Government into its present difficulties. 

The Undersigned 15, therefore, directed to state that no amount of 
loan would be of any permanent benefit to the Porte, unless a system of 
economy and of regularity is intredueed into the various Departments of 
the State. ‘That experience of the past has proved to demonstration that 
such improvements will not, and cannot be introduced by those ‘Turkish 
Agents who have been the cause of the present deplorable financial 
condition of the Porte; and that if the Sultan wishes to save his Throne 
and his Empire from the catastrophe towards which they are rapidly 
approaching, he will authorize a Commission of experienced and trust- 
worthy Europeans to devise and carry into immediate execution those 
improvements and reforms which may seem to them to he necessary. 

The Undersigned is accordingly empowered by his Government to 
state formally to Aali Pasha that, if His Imperial Majesty the Sultan is 
willing to agree to such an arrangement, Her Majesty's Government will
