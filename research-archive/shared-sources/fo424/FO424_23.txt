My Lord, Bosna Serai, November 7, 1860. 

I HAVE the honour to acknowledge the receipt of Mr. Hammond’s 
despatch No. 11 of the 6th October, referring to my despatch No. 50 of the 28th 
August, regarding the disturbances which had taken place in the Herzegovine, 
and the proceedings of Ali Riza Pasha on that occasion, and instructing me to 
furnish your Lordship with further information on the subject. 

I have now the honour to inclose copies of Mr. Vice-Consul Zohrab’s 
despatch to me, dated the 2nd instant, transmitting his report and his inclosures 
on the affairs above alluded to, which he had been specially charged to investigate. 

Your Lordship will perceive from these papers that, if the Turks appear by 
oppression and incapacity to govern to have brought the Herzegovine to a state 
of rebellion and ruin, at all events the atrocities ascribed to them have been 
almost entirely the inventions of those who are interested in representing Turkey 
to the world in the worst possible light. 

An incident which occurred at Mostar just before Mr. Zohrab started for 
Gasko will show how entirely the people look to Russia for inspiration as to 
what they shall say, or how they shall act. M. Moreau, the Chancelier of the 
French Consulate here, who went to Gasko at the same time as Mr. Zohrab, 
happened to be paying a visit to the Russian Consul at Mostar, when a peasant 
was introduced, who not knowing who M. Moreau was, or that he spoke the 
Slave language, told the Consul that the Christians of Gasko having heard that 
English and French agents were about to visit that district to inquire into the 
late affairs, had sent him to the Russian Consulate to ask what they were to 
say. 
I need make no other remarks on the inclosed report than that, having 
personally visited the churches and villages in question, which neither the Russian 
Consul nor Aekee bey attempted to do, Mr, Zohrab’s statement of what has 
occurred, may be considered to be as correct as possible.

The Christians of the district of Gasko, in common with the Christians of 
all the other districts of the Herzegovina, had much to complain of, both 
against the Government and against their fellow. Mussulman subjects; their 
erievances were known to the Government, but a deaf ear was turned to them, 
even after the repeated warnings which were given by the successful revolts of 
Bagnani, Zupzi, Piva, &e. 

These districts were forced into revolt by the negligence of the Government 
in not investigating their complaints, and rendering prompt justice where justice 
was urgently called for. Promises were made only to be immediately broken ; 
the causes of complaint continued in full vigour, and at length the people were 
driven to despair, and preferring death to a life of misery and servitude, rebelled. 
Grahovo was the first to set an example ; it was followed by the districts of 
Zupzi and Piva in 1858, by Bagnani in 1859, by Drobniak in 1859, and by 
Gasko this year. 

In spite of all these indications of the utter rnin which follows the present 
mode of administering the Government of the Herzegovina, the Porte still 
continues the same system in those districts which are yet loyal. It is only 
when tu district rises in rebellion that the Porte volunteers concessions and 
promulgates reforms, and what is worse, shows her weakness by becoming a 
suppliant; a premium is thus set on rebellion: the loyal districts see their rebel 
neighbours not only emancipated from all oppression but respected and petted by 
the Government, while they continue uncared for and oppressed. What 
‘nducements have they, then, to remain loyal ? 

[t is not merely by a change of Governors, or other functionaries, that the 
evil can be got over; indeed, | think a change of the higher functionaries at this 
moment would be prejudicial, as long residence in the Herzegovina has made 
them well acquainted with the people and their wants. It is an immediate and 
entire reform in the mode of carrying on the Government, and of administering 
justice, thut is loudly called for, and if these are not speedily looked to, the

_ As regards the districts in rebellion, enersetic steps should be taken in the

It is a fact, though persons interested deny it, that the Christians of Gasko 
revolted in the early part of the last spring; that they then murdered several

those of Piva, Zupzi, Drobniak, &c., as rebels, and as such, worthy of punishment, 
Their only excuse is, their utter ignorance of law, and of their duty as subjects. 
Their wrongs were certainly grievous, and redress upper’ hopeless ; this, 
coupled with the success which has attended the revolt of the neighbouring 
districts, appeared to justify their opposition to a Government which, instead of 
protecting and dispensing Justice, protected and aided their oppressors. So far, 
these people were justified, perhaps, in seeking themselves a remedy for the 
evils which weighed them down, when the Governinent refused to do it for 
them: but, unhappily, from being the oppressed, a slight success has turned 
them into oppressors; and refusing all concessions, they desire, in common 
with the rest of the revolted districts, entire emancipation from the Turks; and 
all whe do not join them to this extent, they look upon as enemies, and treat as 
such when occasion offers. - |
