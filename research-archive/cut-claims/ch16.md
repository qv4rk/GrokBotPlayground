# Chapter 16 (Valid Against the World, 1849–1856): claims cut or corrected

Copied verbatim from the "Corrections to the original" section of book/16-narrative.md (local narrative rewrite, 50482 bytes). It records what in the original chapter the sources contradicted or could not support, and what was done about it. Chapter text was not changed.

## Corrections to the original

- The original cites "Hansard, HC Deb 14 March 1856, vol. 141" for the Pollok debate. The debates were on 29 April 1856 (vol. 141, cc. 1707–18) and 27 May 1856 (vol. 142, cc. 697–712).
- The original says Disraeli and Palmerston "suggested on the floor of the Commons that Pollok's evictions, and the police support they demanded, were themselves provoking exactly the rural disorder the Encumbered Estates Court had been built to prevent." Neither said so. Disraeli called the exercise of rights described "most intolerable in a Christian nation" and said the court gave strangers the opportunity "for depopulating large districts"; Palmerston deplored "clearing estates" in general and on 27 May said he had expressed no opinion about Pollok. The argument that the evictions tended to disturb the public peace was M'Mahon's.
- The original says the debate "resumed a month later." The second debate, on 27 May, was on Pollok's own petition, presented by Sir Michael Shaw Stewart.
- George Henry Moore's credit to Pollok ("he paid them well and treated them handsomely") was given on 27 May 1856, with the warning that Pollok was about to relet in large farms. No primary source used here records any reply by a Father Colley of Lecarrow; that exchange is removed. The priest named in the debates is the parish priest of Eyrecourt, Father Derry.
- The original's figures for Pollok (£212,460 for 25,234 acres, July 1853 to June 1858) and the 25,460-acre total come from parish and newspaper histories, not from a primary source; they are removed. Hansard gives 7,414 statute acres with 500 tenants on the Ballynakill and Kilbegnet estates, and 246 tenants on the West and Cornborough estates. M'Mahon puts the West and Cornborough estates at 1,200 acres; Maguire, from Pollok's reply, speaks of "one estate of 12,000 acres" and 240 tenants. Both figures are given as each speaker gave them.
- The Eyre family of Eyrecourt and Edmund Dowell's 4,401 acres at Skehaghard are not named in any primary source used here and are removed. Hansard calls Pollok's purchases near Eyrecourt the West estate and Cornborough.
- The original's account of Pollok, Gilmour & Co., its Atlantic timber, the shipload of Canadian timber at Limerick, the Lismanny foundation stone and "an agricultural enterprise on a vast industrial scale" rests on later local histories and is removed. The record of 1856 gives his "vast wealth," his Renfrewshire estate, and on the West estate nine farmsteadings, twenty cottages, a corn-mill and a mansion-house.
- The original compares Pollok's plan to David J. Wilson's evidence before the Devon Commission. No such evidence was checked for this chapter and the comparison is removed.
- The original says "three commissioners sat two days a week" and quotes a barrister on business "in excess of all anticipations." Neither is supported by a primary source used here; both are removed. The conversion of the stable and coach-house into the sale court is confirmed by the architect's memorandum of 1853, which gives its size as 30 by 29 feet.
- The original says the court's title was wiped clean of "every customary tenant arrangement." In Errington v. Rorke (1856) the Irish Queen's Bench held a purchaser bound by an earlier lease not mentioned in his conveyance, and the Chief Secretary told the Commons the decision was "at variance with" what Parliament had intended.
- The original says Pollok "had never set foot on any of it before he owned it." No source used here says so; it is removed.
- The original's source list rests on heritage websites, a parish history, a newspaper column and the Landed Estates Database. All are replaced by Hansard, the parliamentary papers of the court, Trevelyan, Caird, the 1852 Observations and the Annual Register for 1856.
