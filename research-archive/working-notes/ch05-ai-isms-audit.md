## Noticed

> "The same shape turns up later on a different ledger: a peasant in عين الزيتون ʿAyn al-Zaytūn bound to a merchant's book in Beirut before the olives are on the tree. Advance against a harvest that has not happened. Different empire. Same hinge."

Pattern: cross-case resolver ("the same shape" / "same hinge") plus stacked fragments ("Different empire. Same hinge."). It teaches the reader the comparison instead of letting the next chapter earn it. Unsure on fragment density for the rest of the page; this block is the loudest hit.

> "What it cost to make a chest had nothing to do with what the chest would fetch once it left the river. That gap was the point."

Pattern: "X did not A. X did B." / aphorism close. Short punch after the factory paragraph.

> "Indian labor into Chinese habit. Chinese silver into London paper. London paper into British tea."

Pattern: demanded-cascade with parallel slots. Clean rhythm, but it is the cascade form named in the guide.

## Suggested

For the Ayn al-Zaytūn block: cut the "Different empire. Same hinge." lines; keep the dadni fact in Bihar and leave the olive advance for the Galilee chapter.

For the gap sentence: "The factory price and the Calcutta hammer price were not the same number."

For the cascade: leave as is, or one sentence: "Labor in India became habit in China and paper in London that bought British tea."
