Composites and reconstructions:
- The tenant near the Lerr, his wife and his neighbour are a composite. He does only what the record shows small Castledermot tenants did or said in 1903: he got no circular, or got it too late, as Kilbride said several tenants did; he waited for the Castledermot meeting of Sunday, 27 September, which the Castledermot men had asked Heydon to call; he heard Heydon, Keogh, Nolan and the voices the Nationalist and Leinster Times reported; his land is the colder land and the tillage farm under £50 that Heydon spoke for, with arrears that the terms added to the purchase money; he shares Nolan's view on gamekeepers and the voice's view on the Lerr drainage; he is one of the three dissentients, whose names were not reported. The gale of 29 September and 1 November forgiven, interest from 29 September in place of rent, and the annuity of £3 5s. per £100 are from the terms and the Act. The estate office and its book, the potatoes, the tin, the almanac, the field by the Lerr, the forge in 1908, the show of hands and what he saw, smelled and felt are reconstructed. No source records how the vote was taken.
- The scene at the Town Hall, Maynooth, is told from the side of the Leinster Leader's reporter, who is not named in the report; he does only what the report says he did.
- No word is put in the mouth of any real person. Wyndham, Minch, Deegan, Hamilton, Edward and Stephen Heydon, Keogh, Nolan, Kilbride, Muskerry, Cox, Craig, Cherry and Birrell are quoted from the sources above, and reported speech is kept as reported. Where the Castledermot report gives "a voice," the chapter does not give the voice a name.

Material moved: Chapter 08 ends with the Land League's demand, in October 1879, for "the ownership of the soil by the occupiers." This chapter takes up the Leinster estate's own record between 1881 and 1903 from Kilbride's speech of 1905: the 1881 valuation and its 20 per cent reduction; the refusal of a further reduction because of the mortgage; the sales under the Ashbourne Act; the 12½ per cent reduction after 1887. That fills the gap between the two chapters. The chapter also runs past the original's 1903 date to 1909 and covers the Lords debate of 1904, Kilbride in 1905, the Duke's coming of age in 1908, and the debates of December 1908 and March 1909, which the original summarised in its last paragraph. It hands nothing forward to chapter 13. The 1922 sale of the Duke's reversion to Sir Harry Mallaby-Deeley (Leinster Leader, 17 and 24 June 1922) belongs to the 1920s chapters and is left for them.

## Corrections to the original

- The original says the previous Duke "had already sold 19,200 acres in 1885 just to service £290,000 in debt." These figures come from a secondary history of Carton (Dooley and Mallaghan), not from a primary source, and the fifth Duke did not inherit until 1887, so the sale was not in 1885. They are replaced by Kilbride's figures in the Commons in 1905: a mortgage of over £250,000 in 1881, and estates sold under the Ashbourne Act for £343,000, which relieved the mortgages.
- The original says that "by 1902 the trustees were auctioning off the family's Gainsborough and Van der Hagen paintings at Christie's." The only source found is the same secondary history; no Christie's catalogue or newspaper report naming the Leinster pictures was found. It is removed.
- The original says that eighteen men "settled the whole estate's terms in a meeting the Irish Times reported lasted under an hour." The Irish Times said between thirty minutes and an hour. The figure of eighteen is Cosgrove's count of the two deputations from the Return of Advances. No source counts the men in the room on 24 September. The chapter gives the deputation's names and holdings and does not say all eighteen sat with Lord Frederick.
- The original says the Castledermot tenants "did not get a seat at that meeting at all." Edward Heydon and Richard Wright, who held land at Castledermot as well as Kilkea, were at the 24 September meeting and pressed for the arrears of the small tillage farms. What is true is that no Castledermot meeting had chosen them. The chapter says so.
- The original says the trustees informed the tenants "and a meeting was called in Athy for that same day." Kilbride said the circular reached most tenants on the Monday and the meeting was on the Tuesday, 15 September, the day the offer was reported. Both are given.
- The original quotes the crowd as "a voice in the crowd agreed he'd been a good man" and paraphrases the second voice. The report's words are given exactly: "A voice - he was a good man. Another voice - better than the government will be."
- The original gives £766,647 as "the purchase price, the interest, and a twelve percent bonus." The trustees' statement adds £12,021 7s. 6d. of "income" to reach that total. The chapter lists all three parts.
- The original says the seventy-seven largest farmers took "roughly half the entire sum." Cherry told the Commons that the seventy-seven advances over £3,000 came to £381,825, "more than half the total purchase money." The seventy-seven were defined by the size of their advances, which is what the chapter says.
- The original says that within weeks other landlords cited the Leinster terms and that "land prices across the region rose to match." The Mills and McDonnell estates are documented, in the Freeman's Journal of 25 and 27 November 1903. No source used here measured a general rise in prices across the region, and the sentence is removed. Kilbride's 1905 comparison of one holding (£1,800 under the Ashbourne Act against £2,187 10s. under the 1903 Act) is given as his.
- The original places Cherry's words "by 1908" and Birrell's "in a later debate" under a source note of "HC Deb 23 November 1908, vol. 196." Cherry spoke on 8 December 1908 (4th series, vol. 198). Birrell spoke on 30 March 1909 (5th series, vol. 3). The original also calls Birrell Cherry's "successor as chief secretary." Cherry was Attorney-General for Ireland and Birrell was already Chief Secretary. The dates are corrected and the description is removed.
- The original paraphrases Birrell as saying the estate had "never given the government a moment's trouble." His words were "an excellent estate upon which there has never been any particular amount of trouble." The exact words are restored.
- The original's last sentence, that the reform "had instead handed its largest tenants a windfall, at the direct expense of the small farmers in the west," is its own conclusion. The chapter gives Cherry's words on the West and the figures and does not state the conclusion.
- The original says the trustees acted "without needing to ask him anything" and that the Duke "had no legal standing to object." No source used here addresses the minor's standing. These lines are removed. Muskerry's and Cochrane's contemporary objections are given instead.
- The original's sources (Encyclopedia.com, Gale Academic OneFile, Encyclopaedia Britannica, and Cosgrove's essay as a whole) are replaced by Hansard, the Act, the Return of Advances, the Leinster papers and the contemporary newspapers. Where those were reached through Cosgrove's transcriptions, the note says so.
