Shared clock, shared geometry

The Sun, Moon, Earth rotation, surface shadow and observer camera share one UTC epoch. Earth is a WGS84 ellipsoid. Solar obscuration is calculated at every surface fragment from the angular overlap of the Sun and Moon.

The uploaded eclipse catalogs contribute 620 events: the historical collection and the new 1999–2050 collection. At greatest eclipse, the stored Sun/Moon vectors and Earth rotation place the catalog shadow at its computed site. The catalog has no between-event ground-track samples, so those tracks are not invented. The Solar system globe shows an enlarged shadow marker; Earth & shadow shows the physical footprint.

Stars and Western/Chinese constellation figures reuse FeistTech’s Globe & Sky backdrop, with the bundled d3-celestial catalog. The star field stays visible as a celestial reference even in daylight.

Astronomy Engine · Don Cross · MIT supplies positions, true sidereal time and eclipse searches. Three.js · MIT supplies the renderer and orbit controls. Planet maps: Solar System Scope · CC BY 4.0. Earth and lunar maps are bundled from the existing three-globe/globe.gl example assets.

NASA eclipse reference. Smooth lunar limbs are used; this view does not predict individual Baily’s beads. The corona is a visual effect. The 400 km camera is an orbital viewpoint, not a reconstruction of the ISS’s historical flight path.

Visible scale enlarges planets and compresses moon spacing. Earth & Shadow and Sky keep the Sun, Earth and Moon at true scale; other planets appear as direction markers in these views. The existing Galilean moon models and orbital guide curves are retained.
